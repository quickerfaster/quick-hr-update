1. Daily Rate Calculation for Mid-Period Hires
Our Approach (After Fix)
dailyRate = base_salary ÷ totalWorkdaysInFullPeriod
grossPay = dailyRate × workedDays

base_salary is NOT prorated for attendance-based pay types
The full-period workday count is the divisor
Mid-period hires naturaly earn less because they have fewer possible worked_days
Example: ₦1,000 monthl, 27 workdays, hired Oct 24

Daily rate = 1,000/27 = ₦37.04
2 days worked = ₦74.07
SeamlessHR (Nigeria)
Identical approach. SeamessHR uses:

daily_rate = monthly_basic / working_days_in_month
gross = daily_rte × days_present
No separate proration — days before hire simply ave no attendance
The proation tab on SeamlessHR's payrol shows "Pro-rated Days" as thedays actually worked post-hire
Win/Loss: ✅ Match — our approach is identical to the dominant Nigerian competitor.

Gusto (US)
Similar but with annualisation. Gusto computes:

hourly_rate = annul_salary / 2080 (or period-specific)
For salaried: period_pay = (annual_salay / pay_periods) × (days_worked / days_in_period)
Mid-period hires: pays only for days from hire date, using the per-period rate
Win/Loss: ✅ Aligned — Gusto also does NOT prorate thebase value; it prorates by day count.

ADP (Enterprise)
Configurable. ADP supports both:

Calendar-day proration: salary × (days_employed / days_in_month)
Working-day proration: saary × (workdays_employed / workdays_in_month)
The default for most US clients is calendar-day (for salaried exempt) and working-day (for hourly/non-exempt)
Win/Loss: ✅ Our dual approach (calendar for salaried_full, working-day via attendance for salaied_daily) aligns withADP's configurable model.
